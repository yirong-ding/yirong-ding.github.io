About

I am a PhD student in Myers-Lawson School of Construction at Virginia Tech, where I focus on disaster resilience and urban computing. My advisor is Dr. Lu Zhang, and my co-advisor is Dr. Yang Zhang. I am also pursuing a simultaneous Master of Engineering degree in Computer Science, which solidifies my technical foundations and provides new perspectives on interdisciplinary research.

My research centers on understanding and improving community resilience through computational approaches. I develop geospatial machine learning models to analyze infrastructure service disparities during disasters, with projects examining public service inequality, flood hazard mapping, and accessibility impacts from extreme weather events. My work combines explainable AI, knowledge graphs, and spatiotemporal analysis to provide actionable insights for equitable disaster response and policy-making. Recent work includes papers in Natural Hazards Review, Sustainable Cities and Society, and the Journal of the American Planning Association; a Best Paper award at the 2026 CI & CRC Joint Conference; and an invitation to extend my i3CE 2026 paper for a Journal of Computing in Civil Engineering special collection. My research has been supported by the Disaster Resilience Interdisciplinary Graduate Education Program (DR-IGEP) and by faculty-led awards from Virginia Tech's +Policy Fellows Program and ISCE Scholars Program.

I received my master's degree from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, where I worked on an indoor occupancy detection project with Dr. Zhe Wang.

I'm always looking for related collaboration. If you are interested to chat with me, feel free to drop me an email.
